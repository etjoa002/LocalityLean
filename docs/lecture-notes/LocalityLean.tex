\noindent\motiv{\vet{A Lieb-Robinson bound says that in a lattice of quantum systems with bounded
local interactions, information travels no faster than a finite velocity: the commutator of a
time-evolved observable with a distant one is exponentially small outside a light cone. These
notes are the mathematical account of the Lean library LocalityLean. \vetnew{They prove one
theorem, the path-sum bound: under three hypotheses on the generators of the dynamics, it
bounds such commutators by a sum over paths of overlapping interaction terms. We then read off
the published bounds for qudit lattices and open systems as its instances, \revs{and obtain the
bound for harmonic oscillators from the combinatorics of its closed forms};
the chapters on fermions and infinite lattices are to come.}}}

\section*{About these notes}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{About these notes}
\noindent\revs{We assume familiarity with C*-algebras, bounded operators on a Banach space and
their exponential series, and, for Chapter~\ref{sec:harmonic}, the Weyl form of the canonical
commutation relations. After the notation come a reading guide, which translates the generators
and paths used here into the interactions and graphs of the literature, and a roadmap of the
chapters; an index of terms precedes the references.}

\section*{Roadmap}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Roadmap}

\noindent\vet{The notes follow the logical build of the theory rather than the order of the literature:
the objects (nets of local algebras, local generators, paths), the central theorem (the path-sum
bound), its closed forms (site matrix, decay functions, light cones), then one chapter per
instance, each showing the same theorem specialized, and finally the thermodynamic limit.}
\revs{Figure~\ref{fig:roadmap} shows which chapters use which. Chapter~\ref{sec:harmonic} is the
exception to the pattern: the generator of harmonic bosons is unbounded, so the path-sum bound of
Chapter~\ref{sec:master} does not apply, and the chapter uses only the combinatorics of
Chapter~\ref{sec:closed}.}
